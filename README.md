# libbdf

A C reader for [BDF](https://github.com/shibukawa/bdf) (Browser-specific Document Format) documents, for native applications: a core that owns the format, a software rasterizer, and thin adapters that draw through Skia, CoreGraphics, QPainter and cairo.

What exists today is the core's reading side, the rasterizer, and the adapters:

- `include/bdf/bdf.h` — the public C ABI (opaque handles, length-tagged strings, nothing global; see the header's preamble for the rules)
- the single-file and split containers, the manifest (its own JSON reader, the checks of the reference), parts with their deflate-raw decoder, the Object part decoder with its instruction iterator, and path collections — each ported from the reference implementation check for check, with no dependency beyond libc
- WOFF2 fonts unpacked to sfnt (`bdf_woff2_decode`, `bdf_font_sfnt`), byte for byte what the reference's `font/woff2` makes of them, over the Brotli decoder vendored under `third_party/brotli` (MIT; the one dependency, and optional)
- text: the runs of an object and the objects it draws (`bdf_text_extract`, `bdf_doc_text`) with the reference's order, ordinals, separators and limits, the LINK areas and what their urls lead to (`bdf_link_parse`), text index and cue index parts, the text of a view (`bdf_doc_view_text`) and of a document for a search index (`bdf_doc_search_text`)
- the structure the marks give the text, for reading aloud (`bdf_text_node_at`: paragraphs, headings, lists, tables and their cells, figures with their alternative text and bounds, the language of runs), and a search that takes characters for what they are to a reader (`bdf_text_search_new`, `bdf_text_search_find`: width, case, kana, ligatures; hits located in the runs), both as the reference's TypeScript reader has them
- encrypted documents (`bdf_doc_unlock` with the password, `bdf_doc_unlock_ecdh` with a P-256 private key) and documents that come in segments sealed for one request (`bdf_doc_add_segment`), with the primitives they need written here: SHA-256, HMAC, PBKDF2, HKDF, AES-256 with key unwrap and GCM, ECDH over P-256, and form C of passwords
- a renderer (`bdf_renderer_new`, `bdf_render_page`, `bdf_render_region`, `bdf_render_object`) that draws pages, strips of scroll views, regions of sheets with their gridlines, and objects into premultiplied RGBA images: paths with both fill rules, strokes with joins, caps and dashes, clips, gradients, every composite operation and blend mode, groups, soft masks and shadows, and images (PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP and WebP image parts drawn whole, in part and as patterns, turned by their EXIF orientation), SVG images, and text. It is the reference's `raster/imagebdf` ported operation for operation, with decoders that are ports of the ones the reference decodes with, and draws the same pixels to the byte, on every platform and compiler
- text drawn from the outlines of the fonts, with no FreeType or HarfBuzz: readers of TrueType and CFF outlines and of character maps, and the reference's layout (a glyph a character, a simple order for right-to-left runs, a line fitted to the width it had where it was written). The fonts a document carries are used as they are. For text that names its font (documents made from HTML, Markdown or EPUB, and characters an embedded font lacks) the application says which fonts there are: `bdf_fonts_new`, `bdf_fonts_add` (a font file in memory) or `bdf_fonts_add_file`, given to the renderer through `bdf_render_options.fonts`. libbdf does not look through the directories of the system; it finds families in that set as the reference finds them in a system's (the family asked for, one that stands in for it, the generic family, fallbacks for the characters a font lacks), and without a set such text is left out with a warning
- SVG image parts, drawn by a renderer of the SVG that previews meet (the reference's, ported with its reader of XML and style sheets): shapes and paths, fills and strokes, linear and radial gradients, transforms, `use` and `symbol`, nested viewports, clip paths, group opacity, text in the application's fonts, and images in `data:` URLs. Masks, filters, patterns and markers are left out with a warning. An image is drawn into a raster at the scale it is shown at, and then is a picture like any other
- drawing through another 2D library: `bdf_play_page`, `bdf_play_region` (a rectangle of a page, of a scroll view, or of a sheet with its gridlines) and `bdf_play_part` run a page as the rasterizer runs it and hand what it draws to the functions of a `bdf_canvas` (paths of lines and curves, decoded images, gradients and patterns, layers for groups, text as the outlines of its glyphs or as strings for a canvas that draws text itself, soft masks as images to multiply by). The renderer keeps the drawing state, so an adapter is a few hundred lines
- adapters over it, each a header and a source file (`include/bdf/bdf_<name>.h`, `adapters/<name>/`): **cairo** (`bdf_cairo_draw_page` and `bdf_cairo_draw_region`: an image, a PDF or SVG file, a GTK widget), **Core Graphics** (`bdf_cg_draw_page`: a bitmap, a PDF, an `NSView` or `UIView`), **QPainter** (`bdf_qt_draw_page`: a `QImage`, a `QWidget`, a `QPdfWriter`; Qt 6 or 5.15) and **Skia** (`bdf_skia_draw_page` on an `SkCanvas`: what Chrome draws a canvas with, and through SkiaSharp the way to .NET). The last two are C++
- unit tests, libFuzzer targets, a differential test harness in Go that runs every document, part, object, font and text of the reference's test documents through both readers, and a runner for the reference's conformance suite

[examples/](examples/) has programs that use it: a command-line tool, the single header on its own, viewers with SDL3, GTK 4, Qt, SwiftUI and Avalonia, and thumbnails with .NET and SkiaSharp; [android/sample](android/sample/) is a viewer for Android.

[docs/design.md](docs/design.md) (Japanese) holds the decisions behind the layout.

## Relation to bdf

- The format is specified by `docs/spec.md` of the bdf repository: wire format version 1, opset 1.
- The Go implementation there is the reference. libbdf is checked against it, not the other way round: `go/` holds a cgo harness that decodes the same bytes with both and compares the result, and the testdata of the pinned bdf module (`go/go.mod`) are its inputs. `bdf conformance` writes a suite of inputs and expected outcomes that `test_conformance` runs without Go.
- Two things only the reference's TypeScript reader has, the structure of text and the search. For these that reader is the oracle: `tools/structure-oracle.mjs` and `tools/search-oracle.mjs` run it with node over the test documents and write what it makes of them to `go/testdata/*.txt.gz`, which `go test` compares libbdf with; and with `LIBBDF_BDF_CHECKOUT=<a bdf checkout with packages/core built>` the tests `TestStructureRandom`, `TestSearchRandom`, `TestSearchEveryCharacter` and `TestSearchNormalizeAll` run it on random objects and text and on every character as well. A search normalises text to form KC with libbdf's own tables and then folds each character with tables (`src/unicode_fold.h`) that are that reader's `normalizeQuery` asked about every character of normalised text (`tools/gen-search-tables.mjs`).
- Dependencies point one way. libbdf depends on bdf; bdf does not know libbdf exists.

## Building

```bash
cmake -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release
cmake --build build
ctest --test-dir build --output-on-failure
cmake --install build --prefix /usr/local
```

CMake 3.21 or later and a C11 compiler (gcc, clang, MSVC). The rasterizer needs the math library (`-lm`) where that is one of its own, and that its sources are not compiled with fused multiply-adds: it turns them off itself with the pragma of each compiler, and CMake adds `-ffp-contract=off`; do not build it with `-ffast-math`. Options:

| Option | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `BUILD_SHARED_LIBS` | OFF | shared instead of static |
| `BDF_BUILD_TESTS` | ON when top level | the unit tests |
| `BDF_BUILD_FUZZ` | OFF | the libFuzzer harnesses (clang) |
| `BDF_SANITIZE` | empty | e.g. `address,undefined` |
| `BDF_WERROR` | OFF | warnings as errors (CI turns it on) |
| `BDF_WITH_BROTLI` | ON | decode WOFF2 fonts with the vendored Brotli decoder; OFF makes `bdf_woff2_decode` report `BDF_ERR_UNSUPPORTED` |
| `BDF_CONFORMANCE_DIR` | empty | a suite written by `bdf conformance`, run by `ctest` |
| `BDF_CAIRO` | OFF | build `bdf::cairo` (needs cairo, found with pkg-config) |
| `BDF_COREGRAPHICS` | OFF | build `bdf::coregraphics` (Apple platforms) |
| `BDF_QT` | OFF | build `bdf::qt` (C++; Qt 6, or Qt 5.15, QtGui) |
| `BDF_SKIA` | OFF | build `bdf::skia` (C++). Skia has no package: `BDF_SKIA_DIR` is its root (`include/`, and its library under `out/`, or `BDF_SKIA_LIBRARY`), `BDF_SKIA_LINK` what that library is linked with |

Some limits can be lowered when compiling the library (`-DBDF_MAX_KDF_ITERATIONS=...`, `-DBDF_MAX_REUSED_INSTRUCTIONS=...`, `-DBDF_MAX_DECODE_PIXELS=...`, `-DBDF_MAX_JPEG_SCANS=...`, `-DBDF_MAX_PICTURE_PIXELS=...`, `-DBDF_MAX_SVG_RASTER_PIXELS=...`, `-DBDF_MAX_SVG_ELEMENTS=...`): the iterations of the key derivation an encrypted document may ask for (ten million, the format's limit: seconds on a desktop, far more on a small processor), the instructions read again from objects drawn many times, the pixels an image is decoded with (a hundred million, at four bytes each), the scans a JPEG image is read to (a hundred: a decoder goes through the whole image once a scan), the pixels of the images a renderer keeps decoded, the pixels of a raster an SVG image is drawn into (sixteen million, and up to twice that; sixteen bytes each while it is drawn), and the elements drawn of one SVG image (two hundred thousand).

Consumers find it with `find_package(bdf CONFIG)` and link `bdf::bdf`, with `pkg-config bdf`, or with `FetchContent` on this repository. An adapter that was built is `bdf::cairo`, `bdf::coregraphics`, `bdf::qt` or `bdf::skia` there (and `pkg-config bdf-cairo`); or take its two files into a build of your own, beside the library or the single header.

### Packages

Beside CMake and pkg-config the repository has what other build systems and languages take ([packaging/README.md](packaging/README.md) has the table): a Swift package (`Package.swift`: `BDF`, a small Swift layer that opens a document and draws its pages on a `CGContext`, over `CBDF` and `CBDFCoreGraphics`), a script for an xcframework, a Conan 2 recipe (`conanfile.py`), a vcpkg overlay port and a Meson wrap (`packaging/`), libbdf for .NET (`dotnet/`: the bindings `LibBdf`, and `LibBdf.SkiaSharp`, which draws pages on an `SKCanvas`, for .NET MAUI and the like), and libbdf for Android ([android/](android/README.md): `io.github.shibukawa.libbdf:libbdf`, an AAR of the native code of each ABI with Kotlin classes that open, unlock, search and draw documents into `Bitmap`s, and a sample viewer). A tag `v<version>` makes a draft release with the single header, binaries for Linux, macOS and Windows, the xcframework, the NuGet packages and the Android library (`.github/workflows/release.yml`). Nothing is published to a registry yet.

### The single header

`tools/amalgamate.py` joins the sources into one `bdf.h` (the `bdf_single_header` target writes it to `build/single/bdf/bdf.h`; CI publishes it as an artifact). Include it anywhere for the declarations; define `BDF_IMPLEMENTATION` in exactly one C translation unit before including it to get the library, and `BDF_STATIC` as well to keep it out of the link namespace. The implementation is C11, so a C++ project compiles it in a `.c` file of its own. The single header carries no Brotli decoder: define `BDF_HAVE_BROTLI` and link libbrotlidec (or compile `third_party/brotli` alongside) to decode WOFF2 fonts; without it they are `BDF_ERR_UNSUPPORTED`. The unit tests run against the single header too, with the library's warnings as errors.

## Testing

- `ctest` — unit tests of the decoders and of the rasterizer (`tests/`), against the library and against the single header, and the replay of `fuzz/regress/<target>/`. `test_render` holds random drawings with hashes of the pixels the reference draws of them: it is the test that a build draws what every other build draws. The vectors that take a writer or a second implementation to make (encrypted documents, the answers of Go's `crypto` packages and of `x/text`) are written by `go run -C go ./genvectors tests`; the tables of form C and of what drawing text takes of the Unicode data (`src/unicode_nfc.h`, `src/unicode_text.h`) by `go run -C go ./genunicode src/unicode_nfc.h src/unicode_text.h`; the fonts of the text tests are made by the harness (`go/fontbuild.go`), nobody's work; the tables of the SVG reader (powers of ten, lower case, HTML entities, labels of encodings, colour names) by `go run -C go ./gensvg ../src/svg_float.c ../src/svg_str.c ../src/svg_xml.c ../src/svg_value.c`.
- `cd go && go test ./...` — the differential tests. They need Go 1.27 and a C compiler; the C sources are compiled into the package through symlinks (`go/*.c` → `src/`, `third_party/brotli/`), so no CMake build is needed first and Go's build cache follows edits to the sources. On Linux, `go test -asan ./...` runs the same with AddressSanitizer. Positions of text and the pixels of drawings are compared to the bit on amd64; on arm64 Go fuses multiply-adds and libbdf does not, so there they are compared within a tolerance (`GOARCH=amd64 CGO_ENABLED=1 go test` runs the exact comparison on an Apple silicon Mac, through Rosetta). `BDF_TESTDATA=/path/to/bdf/testdata` points the tests at a checkout instead of the pinned module. The reader of SVG documents has its tests in a package of its own, `go/svgdoc`: the document libbdf reads and the one the reference reads are written out as the same text and compared (documents made at random, damaged ones, every value an attribute can hold, numbers against `strconv.ParseFloat`).
- The adapters: `ctest` runs `adapter-<name>` for each that is built, which draws pages built into `adapters/<name>/compare_<name>` with the adapter and with the rasterizer and compares the two pictures (another library's anti-aliasing and curves, so by how far apart they are, not to the byte). Given documents, the same program compares their pages: `./build/adapters/compare_cairo fonts/*.ttf doc.bdf`, with `BDF_COMPARE_DUMP=<dir>` to look at the pictures.
- The conformance suite: `bdf conformance -testdata testdata <dir>` in a checkout of bdf (or the `bdf-conformance` artifact of its CI and releases) writes it; `cmake -DBDF_CONFORMANCE_DIR=<dir>` adds it to `ctest`, or run `./build/tests/test_conformance <dir>`.
- Fuzzing is run on demand, not in CI (the sanitizer job replays the tests instead). libFuzzer: `cmake -B build -DBDF_BUILD_FUZZ=ON -DBDF_SANITIZE=address,undefined && cmake --build build && ./build/fuzz/doc_fuzz fuzz/corpus/doc -max_total_time=600` (targets: `object`, `inflate`, `manifest`, `doc`, `woff2`, `text`, `unlock`, `search`, `render`, `image`, `font`, `svg`, which draws an SVG image, and `svgdoc`, which reads one; `LIBBDF_UNLOCK_SEEDS=$PWD/fuzz/corpus/unlock go test -C go -run TestWriteUnlockSeeds .` writes the seeds of the last, documents sealed for its key). Go: `cd go && go test -run '^$' -fuzz '^FuzzDoc$' -fuzztime 10m` (`FuzzObject`, `FuzzInflate`, `FuzzDoc`, `FuzzWOFF2`, `FuzzText`, `FuzzIndexPart`, `FuzzUnlock`, `FuzzSealed`, `FuzzNFKC`, `FuzzGCM`, `FuzzP256`, `FuzzPNG`, `FuzzJpeg`, `FuzzGif`, `FuzzBmp`, `FuzzWebp`, which decode an image with both decoders, and `FuzzFont`, which draws text in a font with both renderers; and in the package of the SVG reader, `go test -C go/svgdoc -run '^$' -fuzz '^FuzzSVG$'`, with `FuzzValues`, `FuzzFloat` and `FuzzStrings`). Add `-fuzzminimizetime 20x` for the targets that take whole documents: Go minimises every input that reaches new code for up to a minute, and a run that looks stalled (0 execs/sec) is doing that. `LIBBDF_FUZZ_SLOW_DIR=<dir>` makes `FuzzUnlock` and `FuzzSealed` write there any input that takes more than two seconds. The `fuzz` job of the CI workflow does all of it when dispatched by hand with a number of minutes. What a fuzzer finds is committed by hand to `fuzz/regress/<target>/` (replayed by `ctest`) or `go/testdata/fuzz/` (replayed by `go test`); `fuzz/corpus/` is the working corpus and stays local.

The trigonometric functions of the rasterizer (`src/rmath.c`) are ported from Go's math package, which is BSD-licensed and in turn translated from the Cephes Math Library: their notices are in `third_party/go-math/LICENSE`. The image decoders (`src/img_*.c`) are ported from Go's image packages and from golang.org/x/image, BSD-licensed as well: `third_party/go-image/LICENSE`. The tables of characters (`src/unicode_*.h`) are generated from the Unicode Character Database, under the Unicode License: `third_party/unicode/LICENSE`. The reader of SVG documents takes its number parsing, its string functions and its decoders of encodings from Go's standard library and golang.org/x/text (BSD: `third_party/go-text/LICENSE`). Its XML reader (`src/svg_xml.c`) is a port of `encoding/xmlro` of [tinygodriver](https://github.com/shibukawa/tinygodriver), a library of libbdf's author, who gives the port under the MIT license with the rest of libbdf.
